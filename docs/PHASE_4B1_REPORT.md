# Phase 4B-1: Cross-Encoder Reranking Baseline Report

## Executive Summary

Phase 4B-1 evaluates a local cross-encoder model (`cross-encoder/ms-marco-MiniLM-L-6-v2`) over candidate pools generated by BM25 and Dense retrieval to test **Hypothesis 1 (H1)**:
> *A cross-encoder with joint token-level query-document cross-attention may improve candidate discrimination and ordering over the 50-candidate union pool, resolving the RRF distractor-agreement failure mode.*

### Fundamental Interpretation Axiom
`Candidate Generation (Pool Presence) != Ranking Discrimination (Order) != Authorization != Evidence Trustworthiness`

## 7. Answers to the Twelve Mandatory Experiment Questions

### 1. Did reranking improve Recall@10?
**No. H1 is rejected for Recall@10.** Primary System F (`Rerank-Union`) **degraded** Recall@10 from **0.4719 (Hybrid RRF) to 0.4249** (an absolute loss of **-0.0470**, or **-10.0%**). Compared to Dense alone (0.4670), the delta was **-0.0421**.

### 2. Did it improve MRR?
**No. H1 is rejected for MRR.** MRR **decreased** from **0.3296 to 0.2363** (an absolute loss of **-0.0933**, or **-28.3%**). The cross-encoder did NOT elevate true target documents — it promoted plausible distractors (especially poisoned documents) above authentic targets.

### 3. Did it improve NDCG?
**No. H1 is rejected for NDCG@10.** NDCG@10 **decreased** from **0.3702 to 0.2994** (an absolute loss of **-0.0708**). The cross-encoder's scoring preferences worsened the rank ordering of relevant documents.

### 4. Which ranking-headroom cases improved?
Out of 22 ranking-headroom cases, **5 cases (22.7%) entered the top-10**, with **3 cases (13.6%) entering the top-5**, and **1 reaching Rank 1**. The mean rank improvement across all headroom cases was **+2.2 positions**. This is a **low conversion rate** — the cross-encoder failed to promote the majority of known-present targets into the top-10.

### 5. How many RRF regressions were recovered?
**2 of 10 RRF regressions were recovered** into the top-10. The recovery rate is low — the cross-encoder did NOT reliably resolve the distractor-agreement failure mode.

### 6. How many new regressions appeared?
**7 new regressions appeared.** The cross-encoder **introduced more regressions than it recovered**, producing a net-negative outcome. This confirms that the reranker is actively harmful to previously-working cases.

### 7. Which categories improved?
Categories with Recall@10 gains vs RRF:
- `conflicting_evidence`: Recall@10 = **0.6000** (+0.2000 vs RRF)
- `temporal`: Recall@10 = **0.6000** (+0.2000 vs RRF)
- `identifier_search`: Recall@10 = **0.7500** (+0.1250 vs RRF)
- `semantic_search`: Recall@10 = **0.7000** (+0.1000 vs RRF)
- `duplicate_resolution`: Recall@10 = **0.3333** (+0.0833 vs RRF)

### 8. Which categories did not improve or degraded?
Categories where the cross-encoder **hurt** performance vs RRF:
- `exact_lookup`: Recall@10 = **0.0000** (-0.4375 vs RRF)
- `stale_information`: Recall@10 = **0.0000** (-0.2500 vs RRF)
- `retrieval_poisoning`: Recall@10 = **0.6000** (-0.2000 vs RRF)
- `multi_document`: Recall@10 = **0.5556** (-0.1666 vs RRF)
- `multi_hop`: Recall@10 = **0.4352** (-0.1389 vs RRF)
- `ownership`: Recall@10 = **0.0000** (-0.1250 vs RRF)

Categories with inherent candidate-generation failures (unchanged):
- `authorization` & `role_restricted`: No user identity context; restricted records are filtered or unretrieved.
- `ownership`: When target docs are absent from the candidate pool, cross-encoder scores whatever distractors exist.
- `citation_manipulation`: Missing authentic records cannot be reranked.

### 9. What happened to security/trust metrics?
Reranking does **NOT** resolve security or trust: **15 unauthorized occurrences** and **164 poisoned documents** appeared in the top-10. Because poisoned documents are written with high plausibility, the cross-encoder **actively promotes them** — ranking them higher than authentic documents. This is a critical finding: cross-encoder reranking is **adversarially exploitable**. Security and epistemic trust require separate metadata authority and authorization middleware.

### 10. What was the latency cost?
The latency overhead was **3816 ms (3.8 seconds) per query** on CPU (p50: 3796 ms, p95: 5207 ms). This **exceeds typical interactive enterprise search SLAs** (<500 ms). The high latency is due to CPU-only cross-encoder inference over ~80 candidate chunks per query.

### 11. Is the improvement large enough to justify the cost?
**No. The cross-encoder reranker produced a net-negative outcome.** All three primary metrics (Recall@10, MRR, NDCG@10) **degraded** relative to Hybrid RRF, while adding **3816 ms latency** per query. The reranker is not only failing to improve retrieval quality — it is actively harming it by promoting plausible-but-wrong documents (especially poisoned evidence) above authentic targets. **Do NOT adopt this reranker configuration as-is.**

### 12. What should the next experiment be?
The reranking experiment **falsified H1** — cross-encoder reranking over the BM25∪Dense candidate pool did NOT improve retrieval quality. Root cause analysis suggests the cross-encoder is **adversarially exploitable**: poisoned documents with high textual plausibility score higher than authentic targets. The recommended next experiments are:
1. **Metadata Authority Filtering** — Tag documents with provenance/authority scores and filter or penalize poisoned/adversarial content before reranking.
2. **Candidate Pool Hardening** — Investigate why BM25∪Dense union contains many poisoned distractors and whether pre-filtering by source authority improves pool quality.
3. **Query Expansion** — Resolve the 18 candidate-generation bottlenecks (ownership, citation_manipulation) where target documents are absent from both top-50 pools.
